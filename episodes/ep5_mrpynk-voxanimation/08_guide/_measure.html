how it works

One skill, six clips, one finished video

A skill is a set of instructions Claude follows. This one researches your topic and writes six narration lines, one image prompt for the look and one video prompt per 10-second scene. Your generator makes the image and the clips. Claude records the narrator and joins everything into one 60-second MP4.

set up once, about 20 minutes
1

Two accounts. Claude Pro, US$20 a month (pricing). Higgsfield Plus, which includes Seedance 2.5: US$49 a month, or US$39 a month if you pay the year up front, for about 1,000 credits a month (pricing).

2

Get the skill. Comment PYNK on @mr.pynk's reel. His page DMs a link to a Notion page; a free Notion account unlocks the download. Unzip it anywhere, Desktop is fine. You get a folder called Vox Animations.

3

Open it in Claude. Install the Claude app (download) and sign in. Click Code at the top centre (update the app if it is missing), then Local, then Select folder, and pick the Vox Animations folder.

4

Start it. Type let's make a video. The skill checks for ffmpeg and edge-tts, both free. Type install if it asks, and your computer password if it asks once. Then it asks for your topic: that is step 1 below.

5

Connector, do it once. It lets Claude run the Higgsfield prompts and saves you seven downloads and seven drags. In the Claude app menu: Settings, Connectors, Add custom connector, paste https://mcp.higgsfield.ai/mcp, sign in to Higgsfield. Check: type check my Higgsfield balance. A number means connected.

6

Spending. A note in the folder, CLAUDE.md, tells Claude never to spend credits from there, so with the connector it may decline. Open CLAUDE.md with TextEdit and delete its last paragraph. Claude still asks you to allow each Higgsfield call. Each video gets its own folder inside Vox Animations, projects; Claude prints the path.

1

The ask: say the topic, the arc and the voice

2 minutes

Type what you want as if briefing an editor: the subject, where the story starts and ends, the kind of narrator. Ask for three topics if you have none. Claude then prints a short table of settings; type diorama for the paper look in this guide (the default, mixed, is a bright collage). Every 10 seconds of length is one scene. The skill comes back with a one-line angle and six lines of 18 to 20 words, each built on a checkable fact. Read them out loud once. Free.

the ask "Research the most controversial unsolved mystery cases in the world. Give me three topics. From the beginning to the cliffhanger, and end on the part people might never find out. A narrator that fits the mystery feel." It offered D.B. Cooper, the comet 3I/ATLAS and Amelia Earhart.
receipt Six lines, 116 words, two sources for nearly every fact, saved in the project folder for you to check.
2

One image locks the look

3 minutes

The skill writes one image prompt in the paper diorama style and offers three looks with names. Make one as a 9:16 image with Nano Banana Pro on Higgsfield, 2 credits, about 8 cents: say generate the lagoon key, using the name of your pick. Without the connector, Claude prints the prompt and the settings; paste them into Nano Banana Pro on higgsfield.ai, download the image and drag it into your project folder. That picture is attached to every clip prompt, so six clips come out as one film. Pick the look that fits the ending; this story ends in the sea, so the blue one won.

4

The narrator

2 minutes

The skill offers three voices: Christopher, Ryan and Jenny. Say samples to hear line one in all three; it saves the files in the project folder. Reply with the name you want and it records all six lines with edge-tts, a free tool that uses Microsoft's online voices. It then checks each take against its 10-second clip and reworks any line that runs short or long. Ryan, a British male voice, fits a mystery. This step costs nothing.

line one, as Ryan reads it "At eight forty-three in the morning, July second, nineteen thirty-seven, Amelia Earhart spoke her last words into a radio."
5

Claude cuts it

3 minutes

With the connector the clips are already in place; otherwise drop the six downloads into the clips folder. Say done. The skill joins each clip to its line, starts the voice a quarter second in and keeps the clip's own sound low under it. It writes final.mp4 into your project folder at the clips' size, 720 by 1280 at 720p. Then the loudness fix below, AirDrop or message the file to your phone, and watch it once with the sound on.

receipt 60 seconds, six scenes, 720 by 1280. On the night, 40 minutes from first prompt to final.mp4 with the camera rolling between steps; the steps above add to 30 at a desk.

The six scenes of the finished video. One style image kept them in one look; the orange thread runs through every scene because the brief asked for one object that carries through.

If this happens

presetHiggsfield returns a preset suggestion instead of a clip. Say no and send the same prompt again.
429A rate limit after five clips at once. Send three, wait for them, send three.
shortA narration line shorter than its clip leaves dead air. Ask for two more words in that line.
quietThe first final.mp4 played low, about minus 21 LUFS. Ask Claude to normalise the loudness to minus 16 LUFS before you post.
textClips misspell long text. The style stamps one short label a scene, such as 8:43, and those rendered right. Keep sentences in the narration.
1080pWorth the extra, about US$12, only if the video goes to a TV or YouTube. A phone screen cannot tell.

Who this is for, and who it is not for

use it if

You want an explainer, a history short or a product story: one narrator, one look, a clean cut. You can spend about US$16 a video on a generator you already pay for. Swap in any topic.

skip it if

You need your own face, real footage or a brand's exact logo in the shots. Generated clips drift on text and faces. You want it free. The skill is free; the clips cost about US$16.
