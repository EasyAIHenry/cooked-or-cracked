before you start

Why I wrote this down

A creator said five free tools turn Claude into a designer. I did not want to review the claim from my chair, so I picked a real shop with no website and built one with those tools on camera. The layout, the type and the colours came out well. The product shot did not. That gap is the whole lesson, and it is why step 4 exists.

This guide is the part that does not fit in ninety seconds: the prompts as I typed them, the install lines, the Higgsfield settings, and the checklist of what was still missing when I stopped.

Find a shop with no website

Open Claude Code in an empty folder and type the first prompt. This is mine, word for word. Change the city.

Design a 3D scroll website for a local business that does not have a website,
so I can promote their service and menu. Take the style from their look and logo.
It has to feel like a two to three million dollar site, and say what they sell
in a few words. Give me 5 businesses in Singapore to choose from.

Claude searched Google Maps, dropped the ones that already had a site, and gave me five with their rating, review count, logo and a one-line idea for the hero. I picked Pompette, a soft-serve counter in Beauty World Centre.

4.9 from 378 reviews
"Add website": there is none

Pompette on Google Maps, 26 September 2026.

What makes a good pick

  • 4.5 stars or better and 150 or more reviews. Proof the shop is worth the work.
  • No website. Check the Maps card and a quick web search, not just one.
  • One product you can photograph in a single frame. A cone, a bouquet, a cup of coffee.
  • A logo with two clear colours. That becomes the palette for free.

Build the first version

Tell Claude what the page is for, in plain words, and let the design skills handle the look. Mine was one paragraph.

Build the Pompette site. Sections: hero with the product, how it is made,
the flavour rotation (two of four change weekly), the menu with prices,
the maker, how to find the counter, footer. Take the colours from their logo.
Scroll should do something on every section. Dark mode. Works on a phone.

Twenty minutes later I had a real page with the right fonts, the logo's teal and gold, a menu card and directions. And a cone built out of code that looked like a cartoon.

Photoreal product with Higgsfield

Second prompt, again word for word. Higgsfield is connected to Claude through its MCP server, so Claude can call it directly.

Upgrade the cone visuals with Higgsfield. Keep the tone and the fonts.
Separately, design a scan-and-order menu page for the QR code on the table.

Claude used GPT Image 2.5 at high quality, 2K, with a transparent background, and generated one master cone. Everything else on the site is made from that one image.

Premium studio product photograph of a single hand-piped soft serve ice cream in a
golden waffle cone, isolated on a fully transparent background, whole object in frame
with generous margin, centred, straight-on eye level with a very slight top-down angle.

with crisp fluted ridges, a thin curled tip. Classic crisp waffle cone, warm honey-gold,
sharp diamond grid, rolled rim. Soft key light upper left, cool pastel-blue rim light from

Seven flavours from one master

Do not prompt seven cones. Prompt one, then pass it back as the reference image and change only the flavour. Same angle, same light, same size, so the page can crossfade between them without a jump.

Recreate the reference image exactly: same waffle cone, same swirl shape, same coil
count, same camera angle, framing, scale and lighting, transparent background.
Change only the ice cream flavour: hojicha (roasted green tea), warm toasted
caramel-brown with a soft milky tone, velvety fluted ridges.

All seven, plus an empty cone for the piping clip and a cup for the menu. Each one 2.75 credits.

Three things that saved a reroll

Transparent background is a setting on GPT Image 2.5, not a prompt word. Bottom-align every cone on one canvas afterwards so the tips line up. Look at every image before you use it: the pistachio one came back with a faint halo and needed its edge trimmed.

The scroll-to-pipe trick

One five-second clip on Kling 3.0, start frame the empty cone, end frame the master cone, sound off. One object, so there is no need for Seedance here.

Locked-off macro product shot, camera completely still. A smooth ribbon of milky white
soft serve flows down from above the frame, fills the waffle cone and coils upward in a
spiral, layer by layer, building a tall swirl with crisp fluted ridges, finishing with a
small curled tip. The cone stays perfectly still. Soft pastel sky-blue studio background.
No hands, no machine, no text.

Eight frames from the clip. The site does not play it as a video. It shows the frame that matches how far you have scrolled.

That is the whole trick. The page pins the section, and as you scroll it moves the clip's playhead. Scroll down and the ice cream pipes. Scroll up and it un-pipes. It feels like the food is moving under your hand because it is.

One line that makes it smooth

A normal web video has a keyframe every few seconds, so seeking jumps. Re-encode with a keyframe every second frame before you use it for scrubbing:

ffmpeg -i clip.mp4 -an -vf scale=720:1280 -c:v libx264 -crf 22 -g 2 -keyint_min 2 -movflags +faststart pipe.mp4

The site, section by section

Seven sections. Each one does one thing for the shop, and scroll does something different in each.

Hero. The swirl rises into the empty cone as the page opens. One line of copy, two buttons, nothing else above the fold.

How it is made. The clip scrubs while three steps light up.

The rotation. Seven flavours crossfade; the page tint follows.

Menu. Original from $3, flavours from $5, toppings $1 to $1.50. From a published review, not invented.

The maker. A frame from the shop's own Instagram. Ask before you use it.

Visit. The counter is hidden, so four steps to find it, plus hours and a Maps button.

Rules I kept from Taste

Two type families only (Bricolage Grotesque and Figtree). No centred hero. Hero copy under twenty words. Buttons on one line. Dark mode from the start. No raw scroll listeners, everything on GSAP and one smooth-scroll engine.

7

The scan-and-order menu

A second page, phone first, for the little QR code on the table. The link carries the table number, so ?table=4 shows "Table 4" at the top.

Open or closed, live. This week's four.

Cup or cone, toppings, quantity, live price.

Review, remove, name for pickup.

A number. Pay at the counter.

edit every week

One list at the top of menu.js: the four flavours on tap. Flavours not on the list show "Back soon" instead of a button.

not connected yet

"Place order" sends nothing. To go live, post the order to a Google Sheet, Supabase, or a WhatsApp sender and show the number it returns. Half a day of work.

8

The last 40%

This is where I stopped for the episode. Everything below is under an hour, and most of it is a conversation with the owner, not a build.

  • Ask the owner. Name, logo, the maker photo, the menu. Show them the page on your phone first.
  • Real prices and toppings. Mine come from a March 2026 review. The topping list on the menu page is a sample.
  • A cup render for every flavour. Only Original has one. Six more images, 16.5 credits.
  • Topping photos. Five small transparent renders for the chips on the order sheet.
  • This week's four. Set the rotation list, then teach the owner where to change it.
  • Halal status. A 2024 post said certification was in progress. Confirm before you print it.
  • Polish pass. /impeccable audit index.html, then drop a DESIGN.md in and see what changes.
  • Test pass. playwright-cli open http://localhost:5173 --headed, click every button, screenshot.
  • Connect orders. Sheet, Supabase or WhatsApp. Then a domain and hosting (Vercel or Cloudflare Pages are free at this size).
What to keep saying. The layout came from the design tools. The photos came from Higgsfield. Neither one does the other's job, and the client pays for the two together.

What to charge, and who this is for

the site
$1,000
one-off. Two pages, their photos rendered, connected orders, domain and hosting set up.
to run it
$250
a month. Weekly flavour update, price changes, one new render a month, hosting, and you answer the phone when it breaks.

My numbers for a one-counter shop in Singapore. A bigger menu is more work and a bigger number. The pitch: a site that looks like it cost a hundred times more, and they never touch it.

do this if

You can already run Claude Code and read a terminal. You have an eye for a shop that deserves better than a Facebook page. You are happy to walk in and show an owner your phone.

not yet if

You have never run a prompt in Claude Code. Start with one page for yourself first. And if the shop already has a decent site, this is not the pitch. Find one that has none.
