"I think we need to separate."

Gavin's wife told him that after 18 months of him driving Grab from 9pm to 5am.

He was hustling for their future. She was the one living in it alone.

I thought about every founder I know who has done the same thing.

The late nights that were supposed to be temporary. The weekends that turned into years. The people at home who slowly stopped asking what we were working on.

We tell ourselves we're building something for them. But we stop being in the room with them while we're building it.

Gavin changed everything the moment she said it. No more night driving. New routine. He offered to drive her to work the next day. A month of space to think. Then the slow work of being present again.

They just came back from a two-month holiday last year. Travelling the world together.

That's the part I keep coming back to.

The hustle cost him his marriage until he stopped. The life he built after stopping is the one he actually wanted.
