One of the most expensive mistakes I made as a founder: trying to know everything my team knew.

I thought that was how you manage people well.

Know their craft. Understand their process. Speak their language.

What it actually did was make me a mediocre version of everyone on my team, and a worse version of myself.

Here is what I know now.

You don't need to know what they know.

You need to know enough to know they are doing it.

There is a difference between understanding enough to evaluate and knowing enough to compete.

One builds trust.

The other builds resentment.

Your job is to hire people who are genuinely better than you at their craft, then create the conditions for them to do it well.

You are not the expert. You are the standard-setter.

That shift took me years to get right.

But the moment it clicked, everything changed.
