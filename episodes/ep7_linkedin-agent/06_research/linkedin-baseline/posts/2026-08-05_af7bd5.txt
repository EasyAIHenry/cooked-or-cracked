The couple had been awake since four in the morning. I turned up somewhere in the middle, got about ninety seconds with them, and they looked at me like I was the first person they had seen all day.

At my first Malay wedding, I walked out thinking about discipline.

They were up at four to pray, before a single guest existed. Then the ceremony, the performances, and hours of blessings, running until two or three in the afternoon.

The blessings move fast. That is the format. Guests come up, take the moment, take the photo, make way for the next family. A river of people, and everybody gets their minute.

Which means the couple gave that minute several hundred times over. The same warmth to the last person as to the first, eleven hours in, starting at four.

I have produced enough long days to know what that costs. Anyone can be present once. Being present on the four hundredth repetition, when your feet hurt, and you have been performing since dawn, is a completely different skill.

To my friend Aisah, congratulations. Thank you for letting me sit in on something I had never seen before.
