I spent two full days last week on the floor of a Korean steakhouse, shooting from nine in the morning until the grills went cold.

COTE, at COMO Orchard. Two days, a lean team, the smell of Hanwoo beef soaked into every take. I could have sent the crew and stayed at my desk. I did not. Ten years in, running a company I once needed fifteen people to run, I still stand on the floor.

People assume a founder graduates out of the work. Mine keeps pulling me back, and I have stopped fighting it. There is something you only learn holding the light yourself, watching how a plate steams, feeling when a moment is real and when the talent is performing. You cannot direct what you have forgotten how to feel.

The AI does more of our work every month. The generated backgrounds, the cutdowns, the first passes. But the call on whether a shot has soul still lives in the room, in a person who has done this ten thousand times.

That is the whole bet of this new Tristeps. Let the machine carry the load. Keep the human on the floor.

If you are a founder torn between staying close to the craft and stepping away from it, DM me. I have tried both, and I know which one kept me honest.
