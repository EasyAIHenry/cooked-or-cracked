On the twenty ninth I sat in a cinema watching Spider-Man have an identity crisis, and recognised the problem immediately.

Sony invited me to the premiere of Brand New Day. No spoilers. Go and watch it, because Marvel and Sony have put the narrative back at the centre where it belongs.

The part I keep turning over is that the villain is barely the point. The real fight happens inside one person, with Peter deciding which version of himself he is allowed to be.

I ran that same argument for years. Creative or commercial. The guy who makes beautiful work, or the guy who reads the P&L. I treated it as something I had to settle, kept picking a side, and quietly resented whichever one I put down.

Cutting Tristeps from fifteen staff to one settled it for me. I could not afford to be only a creative. I also could not afford to stop being one, because taste is the only thing a one person studio genuinely sells.

So I stopped choosing. The work got better and the margins got better in the same year.

Most founders I meet are stuck in that argument. The creative who thinks selling is beneath them. The operator who outsourced taste years ago and cannot work out why everything went flat. Both are waiting for permission to also be the other thing.

Right now I run a production studio and an AI consultancy at the same time, and people keep asking which one I actually am. Both. Each one pays for the other.

If you are holding two halves of yourself apart and calling it focus, DM me. In my experience that gap is where the actual offer has been sitting the whole time.
