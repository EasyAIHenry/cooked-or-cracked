Today I painted over a studio with my own hands. Not to open it. To give it back.

Reinstatement, disposal, rollers, a skip. Nobody posts this part of running a company. You see the launch photos. You never see the closing ones. Every lean business I admire has a room somewhere it quietly walked away from.

James and I stripped the place back to how we found it, throwing out gear that felt essential the day we signed the lease. Something is clarifying about carrying your own past out to a dumpster.

I have done this at a bigger scale. Fifteen staff down to one. It taught me the thing I keep relearning: overheads are never neutral. Every fixed cost makes you a little more cautious, a little more defensive, a little more likely to say yes to work you should refuse just to feed the machine.

Giving up a space is choosing what the next version of the company needs, instead of paying rent on what the last one did.

The company gets lighter. The decisions get braver.

If you are clinging to a lease, a tool or a team shape because letting go feels like going backwards, DM me. It is almost always the opposite.
