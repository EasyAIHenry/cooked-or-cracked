I've made this mistake more than once.

I chose business partners the way most people choose: whoever was there, whoever I trusted in the moment, whoever seemed aligned enough to get started with.

It cost me years.

Not just in money. In energy, in clarity, in the version of the company I was trying to build.

A student at Nanyang JC asked me: what's the most important decision in a business?

I didn't say product. Didn't say pricing. Didn't say market timing.

I said: who you go with.

Partner misalignment doesn't announce itself. It accumulates.

It shows up in the decision you can't quite align on. The direction one person is excited about and the other tolerates. The vision that starts to belong to one person more than both.

By the time it's obvious, you've already lost months - sometimes years - trying to manage it.

The signals were there before I signed anything.

I chose not to look.

Ask these questions before you start:

What does failure look like to you? How do you make decisions under pressure? What are you not willing to do, even if the business needs it?

The gap in the answers tells you everything you need to know.

Most of us skip the question entirely.

That's the expensive part.
