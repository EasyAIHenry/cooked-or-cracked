I pitched a charity video last week, and I spent most of the prep arguing against everything that makes charity videos feel fake.

You know the formula. An actor playing grateful. A swelling piano track. A beneficiary's face blurred like they did something wrong. We have all sat through it, and none of it moves anyone, because none of it is true.

So we pitched the opposite. Real people, not actors. No script. Film the ones you actually help with dignity, not like a crime-show segment. Let care look like an ordinary afternoon, because that is what real care looks like when nobody is performing it.

It is harder to make. You cannot control every line. You have to earn trust before a camera comes out. But it is the only version anyone believes, and belief is the entire job of a charity film.

Purpose you can feel will always beat purpose you announce.

If your organisation does real good and the videos still feel hollow, the problem is the approach, not the heart behind it. DM me. This is the work I most want to do more of.
