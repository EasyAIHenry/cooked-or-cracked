I'm going to say something most people in business won't say out loud.

Most business partnerships fail not because of market conditions or bad timing.

They fail because two people with different beliefs about what the company should become stayed together too long after the belief stopped being shared.

I've been there.

I spent years managing a partnership that looked functional from the outside and felt broken on the inside.

The direction one person was excited about, the other was tolerating.

The decisions that took twice as long because the conviction wasn't equal on both sides.

The vision that slowly became one person's burden instead of two people's ambition.

I saw the signals early.

I chose not to look. Not because I was naive. Because I was afraid of what it would mean if I did.

Ending or restructuring a partnership is not a failure.

It's one of the hardest things a founder can do, and it's often the thing that saves the company.

The question I now ask before committing to any working relationship:

What do you want this to become in 5 years? And what are you not willing to do to get there?

The gap between those two answers tells you everything.

Most of us skip the question entirely.

That's the expensive part.
