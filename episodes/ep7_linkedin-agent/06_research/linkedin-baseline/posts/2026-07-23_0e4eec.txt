Late on a Sunday night, I got on a call with Stephanie Phua to regroup on creating a house for tired adults.
 
We are calling it the Rest House. A weekend retreat for grown-ups who are quietly running on empty. Small groups, sound baths, real conversation, no phones out for content, nothing to optimise, nobody to perform for.
 
The irony is not lost on me that we scheduled the call on a Sunday night, which is precisely the behaviour the whole thing exists to interrupt.
 
That is the honest tension of building something like this. I am a founder who runs hard, not a wellness person, and I have spent a decade treating rest as the thing you do after the work is finished, if there is time, which there never is.
 
Building this has forced me to admit that rest is part of the machinery, not the reward at the end. The weeks I actually stop are the weeks the work gets sharper.
 
We are still early. But I would rather build the thing I personally need than another thing the market says it wants.
 
If you are running on empty and calling it discipline, this one is for you. What does real rest actually look like for you? Honest answers, not the LinkedIn ones.
