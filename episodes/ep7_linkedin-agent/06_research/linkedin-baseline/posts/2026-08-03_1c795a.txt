At ten on Friday night I was still hanging a tent from the beams of a studio in Rochor. On Saturday, twenty people came and lay under it, and the entire point was that none of them had to do anything. 

We called it Rest House. Two floors at Raw Space, eight hours, phones down. No dress code, no agenda, no business talk.

Downstairs had coffee, books, conversation cards, and that tent under the skylight. A fort for grown ups. Upstairs ran the gentle programme. Opening tea ceremony, a session on finding meaning, gua sha, a sound bath, movement meditation, closing reflections. Every part of it optional, which is far harder to design than a full timetable.

Steph and I have been building this for weeks. Regroup calls, vendor onboarding with Narelle and Kelly, a studio recce, a setup day, and that Friday night that ran until ten. A day built to feel effortless costs about two weeks of logistics.

Then there is the part we have not solved.

We ran it pay as you like. Right call for a first trial with friends and family. Not a business model.

So we have been working through the options. A subscription, for people who want this in their month instead of their year. Vendors bringing their own offerings in, with the house keeping a share. Something held back for the people who built this with us, where contributors earn their access while new joiners buy in. Proper ticketing once the format holds.

Charging for rest feels uncomfortable until you look at the alternative. Free events die the moment the founders get tired. A house for tired adults cannot run on the exhaustion of the people hosting it.

Ten years of producing things designed to demand attention. Saturday was the first thing I have built that asks for none.

If you are running something that started as a passion project and now needs a business model underneath it, DM me. That is the exact conversation I am in myself right now.
