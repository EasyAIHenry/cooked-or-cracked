Last week I stood in front of 30 students at Nanyang Junior College.

They were 17, 18, 19 years old. Sitting in a room wondering what their life after school is supposed to look like.

They asked me how I started my business.

I've been running a production company for over 10 years. We've built campaigns for Google, Sony, McDonald's. At 25, I made my first million. At 30, I was the only one left standing.

I didn't open with any of that.

I opened with the one thing I wish someone had told me when I was sitting where they were.

Because the version of entrepreneurship most people sell young people is incomplete.

And I'd rather tell them what it actually took - before they find out the hard way.
