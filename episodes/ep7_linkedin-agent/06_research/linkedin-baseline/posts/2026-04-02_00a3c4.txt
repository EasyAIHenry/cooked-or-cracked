Creative agencies in Asia are struggling right now.

Not because the work got harder.

Because "good enough" got cheaper.

A client used to need a full team to produce a campaign.

Script. Shoot. Edit. Motion. Copy.

Now they can get 70% of that in an afternoon, with one prompt and a free tool.

The question is not whether AI is replacing creatives.

The question is: what are you still being paid for?

If your value is output, you're already competing with a machine.

The agencies surviving this aren't producing more.

They're producing work that actually means something. Work that requires judgment, context, and taste.

AI is fast. But it has no skin in the game.

Your client does.

I technically stopped selling only production time two years ago; it's a byproduct.

We started selling perspective.

That shift didn't happen overnight. 

But it's the reason we're still here.
