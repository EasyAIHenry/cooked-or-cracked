I woke up the morning after everything fell apart and I didn't feel good.

Not the kind that comes before a comeback story. The kind that just sits there.

I was 30. I'd built a company with people I trusted. I was now the only one left standing.

The first thing I had to deal with was not strategy or finances.

It was clients calling for people who were no longer there. Teammates who had built their working relationship with my ex-partners, not with me. People who needed answers I didn't feel ready to give.

I won't dress it up. I was in a depressive state. There was no recovery moment. No morning I woke up and felt clear.

What happened instead was slower. I just kept showing up. I took every call. I stood in for every relationship that needed standing in for. I explained what I had to explain and let the results do the rest.

I've made my first million at 25. I've hit rock bottom at 30. I've rebuilt from there to double the revenue with a fraction of the team.

None of it came from a motivational moment. It came from showing up anyway, especially when it was the last thing I felt like doing.
