I've started and lost companies.

Not to market failure. Not to bad products.

To something quieter than that.

A company is not a legal entity. It's not a registration number or a set of contracts.

It's a group of people who believe in the same objective. Who move together because they genuinely want the same outcome.

The moment that shared belief breaks down - even quietly, even between two people who are still showing up every day - the company is already over.

The paperwork is just catching up.

I've sat in offices with people who had stopped believing in the direction months before they said it out loud.

The warning signs were always there. The silence in certain conversations. The energy that shifted when a decision came up. The growing gap between what was said in meetings and what was said after.

By the time the belief is gone, you're not managing a company anymore.

You're managing a slow exit.

If there's a conversation you've been avoiding with your co-founder or partner - have it.

Not because it will be comfortable.

Because it's the only thing that's actually worth having.
