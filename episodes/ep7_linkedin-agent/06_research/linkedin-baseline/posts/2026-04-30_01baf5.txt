Gavin drove Grab from 9pm to 5am for 18 months.

I kept partners who had already mentally checked out.

Different circumstances. Same lesson.

We were both paying a price for something that wasn't working - and telling ourselves it was temporary.

Gavin's version cost him his marriage. Mine cost me a near-payroll crisis, $10K a month on an office three people barely used, and a period where I was running the vision alone without admitting it out loud.

The moment he stopped driving was the moment everything changed.

I stopped too. Cut the team. Cut the office. Went from 15 people to one.

Revenue doubled.

The business didn't need more. It needed me to stop adding things that weren't working and actually commit to what was.

Gavin built an 8-figure company after his 18 months.

I'm still building. But I finally stopped confusing motion with progress.

Swipe through his story. Some of it will feel familiar.
