Justin Bieber just headlined Coachella with a laptop and his voice.

No dancers. No pyrotechnics. No costume changes.

Two years ago, he was reportedly on the edge of financial collapse. Cancelled his world tour. Sold his entire music catalogue, 290 songs, for $200 million just to stay afloat. Lost his manager. Disappeared.

Then he came back. New albums. Grammy nomination. And the biggest crowd Coachella has ever seen.

I watched that set and felt something I recognised.

At 27, I made my first million running a creative agency in Singapore. At 30, I was the only one left standing. Most co-founders out. Team dissolved. A $10K/month office that two people barely used.

Everyone told me to hire. Scale back up. Fill the gaps.

I did the opposite.

Cut the office. Cut the ego. Scaled down to one staff member and rebuilt every system from scratch.

Revenue doubled. We landed Google APAC & 3M Asia. 15x revenue per employee in 18 months.

I call it The Subtraction Principle.

When things break, most founders reach for more. More people, more tools, more noise. The sharper move is usually to strip back to what actually works and let everything else go.

Bieber proved it on a stage. I proved it in a business.
