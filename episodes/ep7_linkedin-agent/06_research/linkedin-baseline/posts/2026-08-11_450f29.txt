We are planning a film premiere with no cinema. No rows of seats, no projector, no wall. The audience watches from the driver's seat of a parked Tesla.

The film is Revolt, an AI-hybrid short we made, and this month it goes in front of Tesla, IMDA and a room of media. Instead of booking a theatre, we asked a simpler question. What is the most immersive private cinema most people already own? For a lot of them, it is sitting in their carpark. Two screens, seventeen speakers, doors shut, the world locked out.

I spent a call this week with Kaz from Tesla and Jaze, working out how you turn a showroom of cars into a cinema. It sounds absurd until you say it out loud. Then it sounds obvious.

This is the part of the job I love most right now. Not only making the film with AI, but questioning the whole ritual around it. The story stays sacred. Everything around the story is up for grabs.

Ten years ago I was a poly kid shooting corporate videos for anyone who would pay. This week I am turning Teslas into theatres with a government agency in the room. The work keeps getting stranger, and I keep saying yes.
