30 students asked me one question last week.

"How did you start your business?"

I'm 31. I've run a production company for over 10 years. We've worked with Google APAC, Sony SEA, McDonald's Singapore. At 25, I made my first million.

They expected me to say: spot the gap, take the leap, figure it out as you go.

I said: get your foundations right before you do any of that.

The room went quiet.

Your tertiary years are the last time in your life you get to train how you think, with low stakes.

The cognitive muscle you build then - structuring problems under pressure, managing people who frustrate you and still delivering, holding two opposing views and working out which one holds - that muscle is what business will test, relentlessly, for the rest of your career.

I had a business partner who was brilliant in the room but fell apart under pressure.

I had another who was loyal until the direction changed.

Neither of those situations ended well.

What made the difference, in both cases, was not my product knowledge or my client relationships.

It was how clearly I could think when everything around me was unclear.

I made my first million at 25. I almost lost everything at 30.

What saved me was not a strategy. It was the ability to hold pressure and still make clear decisions.

That starts long before the business does.

Give your brain the base. The rest follows.
