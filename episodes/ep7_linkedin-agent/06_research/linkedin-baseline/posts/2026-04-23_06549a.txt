At 15 people, I thought we were scaling.

We were doing the opposite.

More headcount didn't mean more output.

It meant more of my time managing people who needed managing.

I stopped asking: what are we building?

I started asking: who needs what today?

The company was growing around the team, not around the business.

Everyone was holding the same hammer, trying to bang on different problems.

But we hadn't even decided what we were building yet.

That's the trap most founders walk into when they hire too fast without direction.
Headcount feels like progress.

More people feel like momentum.

But if you don't know what nail you're driving, fifteen hammers just make more noise.

The day I scaled the team back down, I got my clarity back.

The business followed. Lesson learnt of the day, these are what most founders get wrong when they identify late and work with the wrong team.
