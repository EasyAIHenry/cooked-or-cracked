18 months ago I made a decision most people thought was a step backwards.

I scaled Tristeps from 15 staff to 1 full-time person.

Revenue doubled. Revenue per employee went up 15x.

Everyone called it smart. Efficient. Brave, even.

What nobody told me is what comes after the subtraction.

The fear of adding again.

Because once you've proven you can run lean - once you've felt the clarity of a small, precise operation - every hire feels like a risk.

Every new person is a new belief system to manage. A new direction to align. A new relationship where the shared objective can slowly drift.

I've lived through what happens when that drift goes unchecked.

So the idea of building a team again, even a small one, carries weight it didn't used to.

I know what can break.

That's not a reason not to grow.

But it is a reason to grow differently.

This time I'm building the architecture before I build the team.

The systems, the culture, the values - explicit before anyone joins.

The foundation has to exist before the people arrive.

Not the other way around.
